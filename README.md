# Classifying the Limits of Asteroid Impact Prevention: The Threshold Between Stability and Disaster
A research paper investigating the limits in asteroid impact mitigation and developing a **conservative estimate of favorable asteroid impact scenarios**. This paper simplifies asteroid mitigation into a three phase pipeline: detection, risk assessment, and mitigation. Leading technologies are selected with justification to represent each phase, including the **Vera C. Rubin Observatory, NASA's Sentry system, and NASA's DART mission**, respectively.  

Through synthesizing scientific literature, this paper identifies limitations in the chosen technologies and finds crucial factors involved in asteroid impact mitigation which include: **warning time, Torino scale values, asteroid size, asteroid composition, and solar elongation**. **Warning time** was determined to be the most crucial variable that influences preventability. The following table presents the results of the paper:

| **Variable** | **Favorable scenario** |
|---|---|
| Warning time | ~17 years |
| Torino scale | 5 or 9 |
| Asteroid size | 140–150 m |
| Asteroid composition / structure | Weak, low-density, rubble-pile-like structure |
| Solar elongation | ~140°–180° |
